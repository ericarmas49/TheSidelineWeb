Sideline Social Media and Branding Roadmap
Purpose
Sideline is live in the App Store, but the immediate goal is not a large-scale acquisition campaign it’s to establish a credible, active social and web presence that:
Reassures users who discover Sideline on the App Store or word of mouth
Clearly explains what the app does and why it exists
Builds a foundation for a future community of Premier League supporters
Creates a foundation that can scale after fundraising and prior to a broader brand refresh
Generates a signal for early product feedback and audience interest
At present time, social should make Sideline look like it has a pulse, validate new users—not pretend to be artificially large.

Branding Bridge
Sideline’s social launch should use a transitional visual system rather than fully replicating the current greyscale-and-yellow MVP or prematurely introducing an entirely new identity.

Like the Athletic, we want to keep our branding intentionally simple, both to appear journalistic and also provide a neutral-ish presence that allows for a future refresh.
Our recommended approach is:
Black and white as the primary palette
The existing Sideline wordmark and core logo retained
Yellow used sparingly as a connective accent
Club colors introduced when content concerns a specific club
Current app screens shown honestly
Simple, if not retro editorial style
Strong football imagery as usage rights permit
The treatment is consistent with current email direction, and also doesn’t alienate the existing product. It does, however, gently steer toward a cleaner, more contemporary brand.
If Sideline is redesigned in coming months, current templates can evolve without invalidating the logo, editorial structure or content.
Yellow should function as a bridge—not as the dominant identity. It can highlight calls to action, selected interface elements, arrows, scores or key phrases, but we’re not sold on it being a permanent brand color.

Channel Strategy
Priority 1: Instagram
Instagram should be Sideline’s primary visual storefront and the first account to activate. Its role is to:
Establish credibility
Demonstrate app experience
Explain features through carousels
Reach new audiences with short-form video (if possible)
Give prospective users a polished profile to investigate
Recommended formats:
Reels for discovery (as soon as possible, largest reach potential)
Carousels for product education and football discussion
Stories for matchday activity, polls and questions
Three pinned posts that immediately explain Sideline experience
__
Priority 1: X
X remains useful for live football conversation, product updates and direct interaction with supporters.
Sideline should participate as a fan-focused product with a point of view—not as another account reproducing scores and headlines. Posts should be conversational, concise and timely. Us being the 15th account to break news or relay scores will gain us no traction in our early days.

X’s own guidance recommends short copy, limited reliance on hashtags, media where useful and active community management.

Priority 2: Threads
Threads is a low-cost extension of the X strategy. It’s great for questions, observations, feature discussions and lighter football commentary.
Content can originate from the same ideas used on X, but should be rewritten for Threads rather than duplicated. Meta recommends two to five posts per week and notes that replies account for a substantial share of views, making participation more important than broadcasting alone.

Priority 2: LinkedIn

LinkedIn should represent the company rather than the fan feed.
Use it for:
The launch announcement
Founder and company updates
Product-development milestones
Hiring, partnership and fundraising-adjacent credibility
Thoughtful commentary about football media and fan behavior
One thoughtful post per week is sufficient. Take up space now, expand later.

Other Platforms
Sideline should secure consistent handles on TikTok, YouTube, Facebook and Bluesky even if they are not immediately maintained.
TikTok should become an active channel only when Sideline can regularly produce native video at a healthy cadence. Sports content performs best there when it participates in fan communities, creators and trends rather than behaving like conventional advertising.
Guidelines
YouTube can initially house product demonstrations and later support Shorts. A Facebook Page can provide legitimacy and future advertising infrastructure, but does not need its own editorial workload at launch.

Launch Grid
A nine-post launch package is still useful, but the proverbial nine-tile puzzle of old is no longer relevant
A puzzle grid prioritizes the profile view at the expense of individual posts. Instead, we need to publish nine independent posts with a shared system. Together they should create a coherent profile, but each must work on its own.
Publish the posts over approximately 10–14 days rather than dropping all nine at once.
Recommended first nine posts
Welcome to Sideline - A concise brand statement: “Everything that matters around your club, in one place.”
Sideline is live - Product screen, official App Store badge and direct download call to action.
What is Sideline? - A four-or-five slide carousel explaining the problem, experience and benefit.
Choose your club - Use the onboarding screen to show that the experience begins with the supporter.
Built around your club - Explain personalization and how Sideline reduces the noise surrounding football news.
The matchday question - A community-first post such as: “What is the first thing you check on matchday?” Use responses as early research.
Why we built Sideline - A founder-led post with a human voice. This can later become a short video.
What should we build next? - Invite feedback on notifications, news, lineups, match information or other product priorities.
Follow the build - Set expectations: Sideline is live, improving continually and inviting early supporters to shape it.
*Pin posts 1, 2 and 3 so any visitor can understand the brand*

Logo-led Content System
The logo does not need to dominate every post. Repetition should create recognition without turning the feed into a series of advertisements. =
Most importantly, we need to develop and approve templates to make regular posting easy and prevent too much visual redundancy:
Brand statement: oversized Sideline wordmark with one short line of copy
Product frame: app screen inside a clean black or white field
Editorial card: headline, small logo signature and optional club-color band
Conversation card: a strong question with minimal supporting graphics
Recommended logo behavior:
Full wordmark on launch announcements and major brand posts
Small corner signature on editorial and conversation posts
Simplified “S” icon for avatar, watermarks and story highlights
Maintain generous white space
Avoid gradients, keep clean and periodical
Never place the logo over visually busy football imagery without a solid field

Medium-term Plan: 3–6 Months
Following fundraising or when production capacity increases:
Activate TikTok with two to three native videos per week
Repost the best videos across Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts
Engage contingent of group of fan contributors across several clubs
Develop pre-match and post-match content franchises
Test creator partnerships with football commentators and fan accounts
When able, introduce light paid amplification budget, behind proven posts
Update design upon planned brand refresh
Feedback posts educate product roadmap

Content Principles
Every post should do at least one of four jobs:
Explain the product
Express a recognizable point of view
Invite supporter participation
Demonstrate that Sideline is actively improving
Avoid:
Generic score graphics that established football publishers already provide
Overly corporate launch language
Reposting news without adding our perspective
Making the account look like an official Premier League or club property
Publishing copyrighted match footage or photography without appropriate rights

Measurement
During the first phase, success should not be judged primarily by follower count.
Track:
Profile completeness
App Store link clicks
App downloads
Profile visits
Saves and shares
Replies and meaningful conversations
Poll and question participation
Product feedback
Which clubs, formats and topics produce highest engagement
Refine content approach accordingly^
